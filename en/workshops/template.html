01Overview

In two or three sentences, say what problem this workshop solves and what participants take away. If you cannot say it in one sentence, the goal probably needs narrowing.

By the end, participants should be able to

  • Finish a concrete object they can demo or take home.
  • Explain the method they used and why it works.
  • Know what to try next on their own.

Who it is for

State prior experience, age or equipment requirements. If there are none, say “no experience needed”. If there are, be specific so nobody discovers the gap on the day.

Output

Describe what “done” means: it runs, it holds weight, it works for an hour, or it is enough to be a demo prototype. The more specific the standard, the easier it is to judge on site.

Safety boundary

Write down which operations only the host performs, which require protection, and when to stop immediately. Do not rely on verbal reminders.

03Run of show

Write the timeline in minutes, not in feelings. Leave time for wrapping up and switching between blocks.

Time Block Host is doing Participants are doing
00:00 Opening State the goal, output, boundaries and schedule. Listen, ask, confirm what they will make.
00:15 Demo Show the key steps once, including failure cases. Watch and note. Hands off for now.
00:35 Build Circulate. Only step in after five minutes of being stuck. Follow the steps. Check first before asking.
01:45 Checkpoint Stop everyone and confirm they are at the same step. Show current state, check each other.
02:30 Close Run demos, take photos, collect tools. Finish, then explain what they made.

04Participant tasks

Write steps participants can read on their own. One action per step, with a clear finish line.

01

Collect materials and count parts

Check against the list. If something is missing, raise your hand — do not substitute.

02

Build the first working minimum version

Make it move before making it pretty. Note the state the first time it works.

03

Make one change and explain why

Change one parameter or structure, watch what happens, and write down the reason.

04

Demo and answer one question

In one minute: what it is, how you made it, what you would change next.

05Troubleshooting

Symptom Check first If it still fails
Equipment does not work Power, connections, consumables, switch state. Swap in a spare; set the faulty unit aside.
Pace has split badly Did someone skip a prerequisite step? Call a checkpoint; ask those ahead to help those behind.
Not enough material Waste or duplicate collection? Pair people up, or narrow the final output.
Someone is not building Confused, afraid to start, or not interested? Give a smaller first step, or a different role.
Decide in advance what to cut

When time runs short, cut presentation polish before core steps. Write the priority here so nobody has to argue about it live.

06Review

Do this within 24 hours, while memory is still fresh.

Three questions to leave with

  1. Where did people get stuck most often, and why?
  2. Which preparation was wasted, and which was not enough?
  3. What should be removed, kept and added next time?
