Bring your fire.

DIHUOLAB is not a place just for looking. We would rather you came to do something. You do not have to prove you are qualified first—bring a problem, an idea, or a craft you want to learn.

01 / WAYS IN

A few ways in.

There is no single membership process, and no mandatory “maker etiquette.” Find the one that fits where you are right now.

01

Become a member / Make things here

Use the space and equipment, join projects in progress, make things with people from different backgrounds. Starting by fixing a small object counts.

02

Start a project / Find collaborators

Have an idea but lack people, equipment or experience? Put the need out there. We will help you find people who might be interested and help you cut the goal down.

03

Build the space / Donate tools and materials

Idle tools, materials, books and parts might find a second life here. The space itself is a project that keeps iterating.

04

Collaborate & teach / Organizations and schools

Courses, workshops, corporate co-creation. Take hands-on making to more places—and bring real problems from outside in.

02 / WHO WE NEED

What we need
isn’t only “technical superstars.”

A project gets done not because of the strongest single skill, but because someone is willing to fill a gap, try, and explain the process clearly.

Structure / mechanical Electronics / embedded Software / AI Design / visual Education / curriculum Radio / RF Woodworking / metalworking Content / photography Project management Want to try everything

None of these are thresholds. You can learn what you do not know, and start from the smallest task. We care more about whether you will keep showing up than what is on your résumé.

03 / FIRST STEP

Start with one sentence.

No proposal needed. The path below is just to show roughly what happens; in practice you can skip or reorder it anytime.

01

Say hello

Open an issue on GitHub and briefly say who you are and what you want to do. Or come by—if you see someone soldering, say hi.

02

Talk about the idea

What problem are you solving? Where are you stuck? What have you tried? No need to make it sound impressive; the more specific the better.

03

Set a small goal

Something you can finish in one go and see the result of. For example: “make the motor spin,” “print a bracket that actually fits,” “run a lamp-building session with five kids.”

04

Get to work

Use the tools, ask for help, record the process. When it is done, leave the lessons and the pitfalls for the next person.
