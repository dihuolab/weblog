DIHUOLAB is fire
that hasn’t broken ground yet.

When an innovation is still just an idea—or still on its way—it is fire underground. Our job is simple: give that fire tools, space and time, and wait for it to break through.

地火
Dì Huǒ / The Fire Below

Dihuo originally means fire beneath the earth. It does not show off above ground, and it has not gone out. It burns deeper down, gathering strength, waiting.

We call the early state of a maker’s work dihuo: not yet formed, not yet seen, but already carrying force. When it finally breaks through, it becomes something in your hand, a piece on the desk, a system that runs.

01 / BELIEF

Before innovation breaks ground,
it is fire underground.
Unseen, but full of force.

We do not really believe in “wait until the conditions are right.” Conditions usually grow while you work: first a workbench, then a printer; first a small thing, then the nerve to imagine a big one.

So DIHUOLAB is more like a space that keeps growing than a finished product. Equipment changes, projects change, people come and go. The making does not.

Hands-on

Make it

Looking a hundred times is not as good as taking it apart once. We respect drawings, but trust the feel left after soldering, cutting and printing.

Open

Share it

Experience, tools and failure logs are worth sharing. Open-source what we can; teach what we can.

Cross-disciplinary

Mix it

Structure, electronics, software, design, education—on the same workbench there are no borders. Good ideas often come from outsiders.

Long-term

Stay with it

We do not chase one-off hype. Give a project another iteration, a tool another repair, a person a little more reason to stay.

02 / TIMELINE

The fire started small.

No dramatic opening—just one “why not try?” after another. Here are three phases of DIHUOLAB so far; more detailed records will follow as projects continue.

Phase 01 / Spark

Starting from a thought

A few people who liked making things got some tools and a workbench. There was no complete plan—just a plain impulse: can we make this ourselves?

Phase 02 / Gathering

Capabilities grew, bit by bit

3D printing, aluminium structures, electronics, software and AI turned from separate crafts into capabilities that could support each other. Equipment was not bought to sit on display; it was used into existence, project by project.

Phase 03 / Now

Opening the door

The space, equipment and projects are still growing. We are opening the door, waiting for more people to bring fire—and for more fires to meet here. DIHUOLAB is not anyone’s private studio; it should be a shared place for a group of people.

05 / PEOPLE

Who’s here?

There is no fixed “member profile.” Some come with a project, some to learn a craft, some just to sit for a while—and then stay.

Makers

People who like to take things apart, put them back together, or just build a new one.

Engineers

Structure, electronics, software, AI—bringing professional problems in search of cross-disciplinary solutions.

Teachers and students

People who want to move the classroom next to the workbench, or make a real project assignment.

Designers / artists

People who put materials, structure and form into relation, and take the work out of the screen.

The simply curious

No project yet—just wondering what these tools can actually make.
